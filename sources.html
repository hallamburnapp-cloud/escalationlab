Sources & Method

◢ METHODOLOGY NOTE

What this exercise is built from. FLASHPOINT:2027 is a fictional branching scenario assembled entirely from open, unclassified sources: public threat assessments, published doctrine, and peer-reviewed or think-tank scholarship. The 2027 timeframe, the Baltic gray-zone opening, the orbital escalation act, and the Taiwan Strait quarantine are each grounded in scenario families that appear repeatedly in the open literature listed below — but the specific events, personalities, communications, and probabilities in the game are authored fiction.

How the branches were weighted. Where the game rolls dice ("field rolls"), the weights are the designer's structured judgement informed by historical base rates and the cited literature — they are not measured probabilities, and they should be attacked in seminar. Chance sequences are seeded per exercise code so that runs are reproducible and comparable across a cohort.

Known limitations. The engine models one principal per side with a compressed advisor cast; it cannot represent bureaucratic politics, coalition bargaining, leaks, or resignation threats except as narrated colour. Branch trees force discrete choices where real crises offer continua and delay. Adversary seats are written from published doctrine and scholarly interpretation of it, in English translation — an unavoidable interpretive layer. None of the outcomes constitute a claim about what any real government would do.

THIS IS A SIMULATION, NOT A PREDICTION. IT IS DESIGNED TO TEACH CONCEPTS, NOT TO FORECAST EVENTS.

◢ THREAT ASSESSMENTS & SCENARIO LITERATURE

US INTELLIGENCE COMMUNITY

Office of the Director of National Intelligence, Annual Threat Assessment of the U.S. Intelligence Community (2026, unclassified) — baseline open assessment of Russian, Chinese, and space/cyber threat vectors.

NETHERLANDS DEFENCE INTELLIGENCE

Militaire Inlichtingen- en Veiligheidsdienst (MIVD), Public Annual Report 2024 (April 2025; published in Dutch and English) — European service reporting on Russian sabotage, maritime infrastructure mapping, and gray-zone activity that informs the Baltic act.

ATLANTIC COUNCIL

NATO-Russia Dynamics: Prospects for Reconstitution of Russian Military Power — timelines for Russian force reconstitution underpinning the exercise's 2027 setting.

BELFER CENTER

Russia, NATO, and the Baltics: Scenarios for European Security and Living on the Edge: NATO's Eastern Frontier, Article 5, and Russia's Motive — Baltic contingency scenario families and the Article 5 ambiguity problem.

FOREIGN AFFAIRS

Amanda Hsiao & Bonnie S. Glaser, "Why China Waits", Foreign Affairs (8 May 2026) — analysis of PRC coercive options short of invasion, including quarantine variants, informing the Taiwan Strait act.

◢ PRIMARY DOCTRINE — NON-WESTERN SOURCES

RUSSIAN FEDERATION · PRIMARY

Presidential Decree No. 991 (19 November 2024), "Fundamentals of the State Policy of the Russian Federation in the Area of Nuclear Deterrence" (informal English translation, US Naval War College Russia Maritime Studies Institute) — the current published conditions for Russian nuclear use, which widen the 2020 text (non-nuclear aggression backed by a nuclear state; aggression against Belarus). The superseded 2020 text, Presidential Decree No. 355, "Basic Principles of State Policy of the Russian Federation on Nuclear Deterrence" (2020, English translation); alternative translation via CNA — the version in force when the scenario was first designed. Both texts are drawn on in the Moscow seat.

PEOPLE'S REPUBLIC OF CHINA · PRIMARY

State Council Information Office, "China's National Defense in the New Era" (2019 defence white paper, official English text) — Beijing's own framing of its security environment, Taiwan policy, and "active defence," drawn on directly in the Beijing seat.

◢ STRATEGIC THEORY (SEMINAR MODE CANON)

SCHELLING

Thomas C. Schelling, The Strategy of Conflict (Harvard UP, 1960) and Arms and Influence (Yale UP, 1966) — commitment, brinkmanship, the threat that leaves something to chance.

JERVIS

Robert Jervis, Perception and Misperception in International Politics (Princeton UP, 1976) — misperception, the spiral model, and the security dilemma.

KAHN

Herman Kahn, On Escalation: Metaphors and Scenarios (Praeger, 1965; Routledge reissue) — the escalation ladder and its limits.

FREEDMAN

Lawrence Freedman, Strategy: A History (OUP, 2013) — synthesis of the coercion and deterrence literature.

ALLISON

Graham Allison & Philip Zelikow, Essence of Decision: Explaining the Cuban Missile Crisis (2nd ed., 1999) — rational-actor, organisational, and governmental-politics models.

POSEN

Barry R. Posen, Inadvertent Escalation: Conventional War and Nuclear Risks (Cornell UP, 1991) — the conventional-operations-to-nuclear-risk mechanism at the heart of the orbital act.

ACTON

James M. Acton, "Escalation through Entanglement: How the Vulnerability of Command-and-Control Systems Raises the Risks of an Inadvertent Nuclear War", International Security 43:1 (2018), 56–99 — why attacks on dual-use command-and-control raise inadvertent nuclear risk.

RIPENESS

TWO-LEVEL GAMES

Robert D. Putnam, "Diplomacy and Domestic Politics: The Logic of Two-Level Games", International Organization 42:3 (1988).

NUCLEAR TABOO

Nina Tannenwald, The Nuclear Taboo: The United States and the Non-Use of Nuclear Weapons Since 1945 (Cambridge UP, 2007).
